Recorded on the floor at SpiceWorld 2026 in Austin, Andrew catches up with returning guest Stephen Engler, who went from last year’s bar session to this year’s speaker lineup. Stephen’s talk makes the case that sysadmin burnout can be tracked like any other system metric, using signals like rising error rates, slower incident response, and slipping security vigilance.

The two get real about suffering in silence, why so few IT pros speak up—and why the ones who do so rarely see change—and how ADHD, overcommitment, and a phone that never stops paging all feed the problem. On the PowerShell side, Stephen walks through the PowerShell Universal self-service portal he built so developers can reboot their own lab servers and add disk or RAM without filing a ticket. He also shares the not-so-glamorous path to the stage, which included 12 polite rejections before a standing-room-only BSides talk, and wraps with a round of quick-fire questions that puts email, mainframes, and AI girlfriends on trial.

Key Takeaways:

- Burnout isn’t a personality flaw or a soft skill problem. It builds in measurable, predictable ways, and treating it like a metric makes it something teams can actually see, talk about, and address before the crash.
- PowerShell Universal can turn a pile of scripts into a self-service portal. Giving users safe, scoped access to routine fixes like reboots and resource bumps cuts ticket volume and frees the sysadmin for higher-value work.
- The first submission is the hardest step. Rejection usually isn’t personal, and once you’ve put your work out there once, pitching the next talk, class, or idea to your boss gets dramatically easier.

Guest Bio:

Stephen Engler is a systems administrator, speaker, and owner of Ctrl-Alt-GenX. His work explores IT operations, security, AI, and the human side of technology. A returning PowerShell Podcast guest, Stephen spoke at SpiceWorld 2026 about treating sysadmin burnout as a measurable operational risk.
