Andrew heads to Traverse City, Michigan for the MAEDS Fall Conference and grabs a hallway-fresh conversation with two Michigan K-12 favorites, Chris Thomas and Microsoft MVP Jim Tyler. Chris kicks things off talking about the mental health session he co-presented this year (rebranded as “You Are Not Alone”) and his upcoming talk on why PowerShell fundamentals still matter in a world of AI-assisted scripting. Get-Help, Get-Command, Get-Member, and Get-Module are still your best friends, even when an assistant is writing half the code.

Jim joins the conversation with a recap of his session on defense in depth for agentic AI, walking through how agents put new pressure on every layer of security, from the network perimeter to identity, email, data, and recovery. From there, the three dig into the real juggling act of community leadership: school boards, planning commissions, rec councils, youth sports, conference boards, and knowing when to step back so someone else can step up. They close with advice for people early in their K-12 IT careers, including seeking discomfort, asking for conference scholarships, building projects that make your team’s life easier, and asking your question out loud because someone else in the room probably needs the answer too.

Key Takeaways:

- AI can write the script, but you still need to read it. Fundamentals like Get-Help, Get-Command, Get-Member, and Get-Module are what let you troubleshoot AI-generated code, and if AI helped write your pull request, you should be its first reviewer.
- Community work refills the tank, but only up to a point. Saying yes gets easier as you get better at things, so watch for the tipping point, pass the torch when it’s time, and build things (scripts and organizations alike) so the next person can pick them up.
- Put yourself out there. Seek discomfort, ask about scholarships even when none are advertised, submit that session, and ask questions in the room. With a wave of Michigan ed tech leaders nearing retirement, the next generation needs to be ready to step in.

Guest Bio:

Chris Thomas is an Endpoint and Cloud Systems Architect at Ingham Intermediate School District in Michigan, supporting multiple K-12 districts with more than two decades in educational IT. He is a MAEDS board member and has been a frequent presenter at the conference since 2014, speaking on PowerShell, automation, identity management, and mental health in IT.

Jim Tyler is the Director of Technology at Niles Community Schools in Michigan and a Microsoft MVP. He is the author of PowerShell for Systems Engineers, the creator of PowerShell.News, and an elected school board member in Watervliet, along with serving on his local planning commission and rec council. Jim regularly presents at MAEDS on security, AI, and PowerShell.
